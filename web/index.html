Limits & cautions

Source

Built from English-Wikipedia "Opinion polling for the … election" articles. Each row carries (i) the source URL of the original poll publication as cited in the Wikipedia cell and (ii) a pinned ?oldid=<revid> permalink to the Wikipedia revision. Polls inherit Wikipedia's CC-BY-SA 4.0 licence.

Coverage

Italy, UK, Germany, Spain, Portugal span 2005–2026 densely. Smaller countries (BE, NL, DK, FI, AT, IE, MT) cover only their most recent 1–2 cycles. Greece covers all elections including the May/June pairs of 2012 and 2023. France presidential is candidate-keyed; French legislative is sparser. Luxembourg has no Wikipedia polling article (LU tab is empty). Cyprus has only the 2023 presidential cycle on EN Wikipedia.

Validation

The Italian scrape was cross-checked against the official Italian government poll register (via the ondata mirror of sondaggipoliticoelettorali.it) on 2,523 overlapping (year-month × pollster × party) cells:

  • 92.9% agree within ±0.5 percentage points
  • 96.9% within ±1.0 pp
  • 98.9% within ±2.0 pp
  • Mean absolute discrepancy: 0.21 pp; median 0.05 pp.

The other countries have not been validated against an external source.

Reported quantity

All values are voting-intention vote shares on valid votes (excluding "don't know" and blank/spoilt where Wikipedia reports them separately, those columns are renormalized to 100%). UK MRP polls reporting seat projections rather than vote shares are detected by their values exceeding 100% and dropped at parse time.

Smoothing

The line on the chart is a 21-day rolling mean per party; the faded dots show individual polls. There is no model-based smoothing (no LOESS / Gaussian process). Series visibility can be toggled by clicking the party chips.

Party identities

Each poll-party row carries a stable Party Facts id (~97% of voting-intention rows). Chart labels and aggregation are keyed by Party Facts' canonical English name for each party — so variant Wikipedia shorts that map to the same Party Facts id (e.g. Italy's LN and Lega) collapse onto one continuous chart line. Hover tooltips give the native-language name. Parties not yet in Party Facts (~3%; e.g. ES Sumar, IT AVS) fall back to their Wikipedia short label.
